One app, six documents

Pick the report at the start. The app builds the right document around it.

Every type shares the same inspection engine, so nothing is re-learned. What changes is the paperwork: an appraisal picks up the client letter, the purpose and scope page, the options schedule and the conclusion of value, while a PPI stays a clean, color-coded inspection.

PPI

Pre-Purchase Inspection

The classic buyer's report: status legend, condition grid, photos, and a price or price range at the foot of the document.

Total loss

Total Loss Appraisal

Locked to a single actual cash value, because a carrier settles on one number. The range control is hidden so a spread can never reach a settlement.

Dim. value

Diminished Value

Minimum diminished value and pre-loss fair market value are both in the value-type list, with the comparables table to support the figure.

Classic

Classic Vehicle

Starts nationwide, because a modified or collector car only has a market nationally. Built for an options schedule as long as the car deserves.

Arbitration

Auction Arbitration Appraisal

For a claim against the selling auction. Captures the sale and disclosure record — auction, lane and run, sale date, consignor, buyer, purchase price, the announced light and what was announced — then the findings, the cost to correct and where that estimate came from. Prints a Basis of the Arbitration Claim page and carries its own Cost to Correct Undisclosed Condition value type.

Finance

Finance Appraisal

Addressed to the lender or client by name, with the appraisal letter, provisions and stipulations, and signature page included.

On an arbitration report the app records what was announced and what you found, and states plainly that whether a condition is arbitrable — and within what window — is set by the selling auction's own policy, not by the appraisal. The document supports the claim; it doesn't presume to decide it.

Illustration of a completed search. Real results depend on what is advertised at the time.

You choose the market

Local, regional or nationwide

Fair market value for an everyday car is a local number; a collector car only has a market nationally. Pick the scope per report and name the city, state or ZIP where the vehicle is — not where you are. The scope you chose prints under the comps table, because the search area is part of the methodology of a signed report.

Links are earned, not typed

Every address is opened and checked

A link only survives if it came back from a real search result and the server actually opened it. Anything that 404s, redirects away, or says the car found a new home is dropped before the PDF exists. Sites that refuse automated checks are held back rather than printed on a guess — and you can override any single row yourself.

On the finished report

Numbered, clickable, dated

The comps print as a table with a numbered source on each row and the full addresses listed underneath, each clickable and stamped with the date it was confirmed. Prices, mileage and sources still print even when a link is withheld, so a comparable is never lost.

Value types

Seven to choose from, including actual cash value, replacement value, pre-loss fair market value and minimum diminished value.

Guides you cited

Add published ranges — Kelley Blue Book, J.D. Power, NADA, Hagerty, Manheim — and they print beneath the value as your supporting references.

One tap to the figure

Average the comps into a single value, or turn them into a low-high range. Or leave the value blank and the report states no figure at all.

Repeat searches are free

About 10¢ of API usage per search, and the same vehicle within 30 minutes reuses the last result at no cost.

Before you ask

Questions we get every week

Is this a subscription? +

No. $499 once, and the platform is yours. There is no monthly fee, no per-report fee and no seat licensing — put as many inspectors on it as you like.

How does the Market Value research actually find comps? +

You set the search area, then tap once. The app searches the web for currently advertised vehicles and completed sale records that match, and returns the description, mileage, price and source for each. Then it opens every address to confirm the listing is still there. Only confirmed links print, numbered and clickable, with the date they were checked — and you can re-check them all with one tap right before you generate the PDF.

Why would a link be withheld from my report? +

Because for a signed report, a missing link costs less credibility than a dead one. Some marketplaces delete a vehicle page the day the car sells, and some refuse automated checks entirely — so if the app cannot open a page itself, it prints the comparable by source and price and holds the address back. You can override any row you have opened yourself, or switch unconfirmed links on for all of them.

Do I have to state a value on every report? +

No. Leave the value blank and no figure appears anywhere on the document — useful when you would rather not put a number in front of the customer. Total loss reports are the one exception in the other direction: they state a single actual cash value rather than a range, because that is what a carrier settles on.

Can I put my own company on the reports? +

Yes — logo, business name, address, phone, website, disclaimer and an About page in your own words, on every page of every report. The checklist is yours to rewrite too, so the document reads like your process rather than someone else's template.

What if the vehicle has no cell signal? +

Work is saved as a draft you can resume, so a bad-signal garage is not a lost inspection. Generating the PDF, decoding a VIN and researching comps all need a connection.
